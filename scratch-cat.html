How the wave is made

  • Two costumes, nothing else. The cat has one drawing with the paw out (wave-down) and one with it up (wave-up). next costume inside a forever loop flips between them; your eye fills in the movement that was never drawn.
  • The wait is the animation. wait 0.2 seconds is about five flips a second. Drag the slider: past 0.3 s the cat looks like it is signalling, under 0.1 s the two poses smear into one blurry paw.
  • Scratch cannot rotate half a sprite. For the smooth version the arm has to be its own sprite, with its rotation centre dragged onto the shoulder, turned a couple of degrees per frame — that is the second script.
  • The rest is secondary motion. Body bob, tail sway and a blink every few seconds run on their own timings. None of it is the wave, all of it is why the wave reads as a cat.

Reduced motion is honoured: nothing starts on its own, and the bob, tail and blink stay still even while the paw waves.
